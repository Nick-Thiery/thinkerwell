| Much of the money came from governments; in the United States, a military agency paid for AI work at universities such as MIT and Stanford | [National Research Council, chapter 9](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/6323/chapter/11); Toosi and others; Agar | 1999 (read 8 November 2026); 2022; 2020 | NRC: “DARPA provided the bulk of the nation's support for AI research” from the 1960s into the 1990s, and “DARPA continued to invest in AI research at CMU, MIT, Stanford, and SRI”. | In 1973, a British report criticised much AI research, and Britain then cut funding for some of it | [Jon Agar, BJHS 53(3), 2020, author's copy](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10105726/3/Agar_What%20is%20science%20for,%20the%20Lighthill%20report%20and%20the%20purpose%20of%20artificial%20intelligence%20research,%20revised%20for%20BJHS.pdf) | BJHS 53(3), 289–310, 2020 | Lighthill submitted the review in March 1972; the SRC published it in 1973. | Expert systems, built from rules that experts gave, began in the 1960s (timeline: DENDRAL, 1965) | [Computer History Museum timeline](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/ai-robotics/) | Read 8 November 2026 | A Stanford team (Feigenbaum, Lederberg, Djerassi) “creates DENDRAL, the first ‘expert system’”; it applied a battery of “if-then” rules to identify the structure of organic compounds. |
