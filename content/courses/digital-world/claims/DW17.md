# DW17 Project: design an AI helper: claim record

Teacher-facing. Not shown to learners. The evidence is real (two projects); the activity's Maple Street starter problems are made up and say so. Checked 8 November 2026 unless a row says otherwise.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eco Pulse was built by three 15-year-old students at SMK Sultan Ismail, a secondary school in Johor, Malaysia | [Samsung Malaysia newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/my/15-years-old-students-from-johor-bags-most-innovative-award-in-samsung-solve-for-tomorrow-2024) | 11 December 2024; re-opened 8 November 2026 | Team "Terralnnovators" (spelled so; probably TerraInnovators), three 15-year-olds from SMK Sultan Ismail, Johor. |
| The students wanted to make recycling quicker and easier; the app looks at a photo of a recyclable item and estimates its weight and value | Same | Same | "AI-driven image recognition app designed to streamline the recycling process", which estimates the value and weight of recyclable items "with precision". Who the intended users are is not stated. |
| It was built for a 2024 student competition run by Samsung with Universiti Malaya, and won first prize in the competition's recycling category | Same | Same | Sixth edition of Solve for Tomorrow, with Universiti Malaya, for students aged 13 to 15; 115 teams; final in Kuala Lumpur on 7 December 2024. First Prize in the "Accessible Recycling" category (Samsung products worth up to RM28,000); the headline calls it the "Most Innovative Award". |
| It is a prototype, a first working version | Same | Same | All finalist teams presented "STEM prototypes" at the finale. No deployment is reported. |
| The organiser says it is precise, but no test results have been published; a prize doesn't show how well it works or that people use it | Same | Same | The only performance claim is "with precision"; no test data, accuracy figures, users, deployment or measured impact. An organiser's press release, not an independent evaluation. |
| People with diabetes need regular eye checks because the illness can harm their eyes, and checking every eye photo takes many trained people | [Gunasekeran and others, NEJM AI (SMU record)](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/9566) | Online 19 November 2024; opened 8 November 2026 | Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of blindness in working-age adults; yearly screening is recommended but hard to staff in primary care; Singapore's programme uses trained non-clinician graders. |
| SELENA+ was built by eye doctors and scientists at the Singapore Eye Research Institute and the Singapore National Eye Centre, with computer scientists from the National University of Singapore | [SNEC, AI and digital health](https://www.snec.com.sg/research-innovation/research-groups-platforms/research-groups/AI-digital-health); [Ting and others, JAMA (Duke record)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1293398) | Both opened 8 November 2026 | SNEC lists SELENA+ under the SERI AI and Digital Health Research Group (ophthalmologists, vision scientists and computer scientists; members include the NUS School of Computing). The JAMA authors include SNEC/SERI clinicians and NUS computer scientists (Hsu, Lee). |
| It looks at photos of the back of the eye and marks the ones an eye specialist should see | SNEC page; JAMA record (above) | Same | A deep learning system that analyses retinal images for diabetic retinopathy, possible glaucoma and age-related macular degeneration ("3 major blinding eye conditions"); it identifies referable cases. |
| In 2017 the team reported tests on about 500,000 eye photos from Singapore and other countries | JAMA record; SNEC page (above) | JAMA 318(22), 12 December 2017 | 494,661 retinal images in total for training and validation; primary validation in the Singapore National Diabetic Retinopathy Screening Program (14,880 patients); 10 further multiethnic datasets (40,752 images for referable DR); SNEC: "approximately 500,000 retinal images", "10 external validation datasets" with varying cameras, races and age groups. Referable DR: AUC 0.936, sensitivity 90.5%, specificity 91.6% (teacher-level detail; not in learner text). The authors: "further research is necessary to evaluate the applicability of the DLS in health care settings". "From Singapore and other countries" is kept from Lesson 10's 1 November reading of the abstract; the Duke record doesn't name the countries. |
| In 2024 the team reported that it was being used in Singapore's national eye-screening programme | NEJM AI (above) | November 2024 | The developers' own case study: SELENA+ deployed within the Singapore Integrated Diabetic Retinopathy Programme, with medical device regulatory approval and shown to be cost-effective. Pilot of the first 1,712 patients: referable DR sensitivity 94.7% and specificity 82.2%, against human graders' 98.9% and 97.2%; higher sensitivity for vision-threatening DR (teacher note only). |
| These are the team's own reports, and they don't yet show whether patients' eyesight is better | JAMA record; NEJM AI (above) | Same | Neither reports patient outcomes; the JAMA authors call for research on whether it improves vision outcomes. SNEC's page says it was approved by the "Health Service Authority" (probably the Health Sciences Authority) and has a CE mark; the lesson makes no regulatory claim to learners. |
| Proposal, prototype, testing, use and impact are different stages, each needing its own evidence | Brief, section 8 ("clearly distinguish proposals, prototypes, validation, deployment, and demonstrated impact") | — | A teaching frame, not a sourced claim. |
| Test with examples it has never seen, from the people who will use it | Lessons 3 and 10 (unseen testing; testing with the people a tool will serve) | — | A teaching point carried over from earlier lessons. |
| The project follows UNESCO's AI system design aspect (teacher note) | [UNESCO, AI competency framework for students](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students) | 2024; kept, not re-opened | The framework's four aspects include AI system design; the lesson claims a partial mapping only, not UNESCO approval. |
