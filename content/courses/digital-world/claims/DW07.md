# Lesson 7 (DW07, `dw-ai-and-languages`): claim record

Teacher-facing; not shown to learners. Checked 8 November 2026 (earlier check 1 November 2026). Harbourside and its Languages A, B and C are made up and are not listed here.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Most big AI tools learned mainly from English | [SEA-LION paper (arXiv 2504.05747)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05747); [Stanford Report](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/05/digital-divide-ai-llms-exclusion-non-english-speakers-research) | v4, 30 October 2025; 2025 | Paper: “the majority of LLM research and development remains English-centric, leaving low-resource languages such as those in the Southeast Asian (SEA) region underrepresented.” Stanford: English is served well. |
| How much writing a language has depends on history: which languages schools teach in, which ones people publish in, which ones governments use, which ones people type online | [UNESCO Languages Matter, as reported by the UNESCO Chair at Universitat Pompeu Fabra](https://www.upf.edu/web/catedra-unesco-politiques-multilinguisme/noticies/-/asset_publisher/BPgktkPOwCtO/content/new-unesco-report-calls-for-multilingual-education-with-strong-emphasis-on-minoritized-languages/maximized) | 21 February 2025 | 40% of people lack education in their native or most fluent language (up to 90% in some low- and middle-income countries); the report stresses educational resources in more languages. The chain from schools, publishing, government and online use to the writing available is the lesson's explanation, not a measured result. UNESCO's own page was not opened. |
| Masakhane says rule by other countries in the past did great harm to African languages, and technology still shows it | [Civic Tech Guide: Masakhane](https://civictech.guide/projects/masakhane) | Undated listing | “The tragic past of colonialism has been devastating for African languages”, leaving technology unable to recognise local names, cultures, places and history. Presented as Masakhane's view. |
| A low-resource language has little digital material computers can use for a task; the name is about data, not the language or its speakers | Stanford Report (above) | 2025 | “Languages with limited amounts of computer-readable data about them.” “Not about worth” is the lesson's framing, consistent with the definition. |
| About 200 million people speak Swahili, but Stanford researchers say there is not enough Swahili writing online for AI to learn well | Stanford Report (above) | 2025 | Koyejo: “Swahili has 200 million speakers but lacks sufficient digitized resources for AI models to learn from.” The Report page shows no date in its text (dated from its URL, May 2025). |
| In 2025, they found big AI tools worked well in English, less well in Vietnamese and worse in Nahuatl | Stanford Report (above) | 2025 | LLMs underperform for 97 million Vietnamese speakers and worse for 1.5 million Nahuatl speakers. |
| Less training data is one possible reason for a gap, not the only one; how a tool is built and tested matters too | Stanford Report; [Suresh and Guttag (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10002) | 2025; 2021 | Stanford names data quantity and quality; Suresh and Guttag list several other sources of harm (learning, evaluation, deployment). |
| Translating from English doesn't fix it all; it can miss how people really speak | Stanford Report (above) | 2025 | “Translation is scalable, but it doesn't capture the nuance of the way language is spoken and written.” |
| AI Singapore, a programme paid for by the government, builds SEA-LION | SEA-LION paper (above) | v4, 30 October 2025 | Authors from AI Singapore (NUS) and NTU; “supported by the National Research Foundation, Singapore, under its National Large Language Models Funding Initiative.” |
| Its first models were trained from scratch; later ones took open models from other companies and taught them more languages of the region | [AI Singapore on GitHub](https://github.com/aisingapore/sealion) | Read 8 November 2026 | v1 (3B, 7B) “pre-trained from scratch”; v2 on Llama 3; v3 on Gemma 2 and Llama 3.1; v4 on Gemma 3, Apertus, Qwen3; v4.8 on NVIDIA Nemotron-3 (said to cover 13 regional languages). Dates are not given for most versions. |
| A 2025 paper by its team says they support 11 languages, such as Burmese, Khmer, Lao, Tamil and Thai | SEA-LION paper (above) | v4, 30 October 2025 | “Supports 11 SEA languages, namely English, Chinese, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Malay, Thai, Burmese, Lao, Filipino, Tamil, and Khmer.” |
| With partners in Thailand and the Philippines, the team also collects writing and speech in the region's languages | [AI Singapore: SEALD](https://aisingapore.org/aiproducts/southeast-asian-languages-in-one-network-data-seald/) | Published 11 March 2024; modified 17 June 2025 | Project SEALD, AI Singapore with Google Research; partners VISTEC (Thailand), Ateneo and XFORM (Philippines); “a variety of text and speech formats” in Indonesian, Malay, Tamil, Burmese, Filipino, Vietnamese, Thai, Lao and Khmer. Its data release was still “imminent”. |
| Covering a language is not the same as working well in it; the paper's main tests covered only four languages | SEA-LION paper (above) | v4, 30 October 2025 | “SEA-HELM is a robustness benchmark, but only covers four languages.” |
| In November 2024, two companies in Jakarta launched Sahabat-AI for Bahasa Indonesia, Javanese and Sundanese; its makers said it was at an early stage | [GoTo press release](https://www.gotocompany.com/en/news/press/goto-launches-sahabat-ai-enhancing-its-leadership-in-indonesias-technology-sector) | 15 November 2024 (dateline Jakarta, 14 November) | GoTo with Indosat Ooredoo Hutchison; 8B and 9B open models; Batak and Balinese planned; “leading performance” against similar open models (no scores given); “still in the early stage of development”. |
| In 2025, they added Balinese and Batak, working with universities and news groups | [GoTo press release](https://www.gotocompany.com/en/news/press/sahabat-ai-gets-smarter-indosat-and-goto-launch-new-70-billion-parameter-model-with-multilingual-chat-service) | 2 June 2025 | 70-billion-parameter model; Bahasa Indonesia, Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese and Bataknese; six universities and four media groups (Kompas, Republika, Tempo, Hukumonline). Developer's description, not an independent test. |
| Speakers can help a tool fit their community, but people still need to test it | SEA-LION paper; Stanford Report (above) | 2025 | The paper's limits on testing; Stanford on community-built models. A general conclusion, not a measured result. |

Not confirmed: SEA-LION's own About page (sea-lion.ai/about) could not be opened through the approved route on 8 November 2026, so nothing in the lesson rests on it.
