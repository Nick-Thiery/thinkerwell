# DW10 AI in your community — claim record

Teacher-facing; not shown to learners. Checked 8 November 2026 (Justin's brief of 4 November). The lesson now teaches one real regional case, Traffy Fondue (Thailand); there is no fictional material. The made-up town of Sunvale and SELENA+ were removed in this revision (their rows from the 1 November record are kept at the end for reference).

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Researchers at NECTEC, part of Thailand's national science agency, NSTDA, built Traffy Fondue. | [NSTDA: Thailand's AI-powered citizen report platform](https://www.nstda.or.th/en/news/news-years-2026/thailand’s-ai-powered-citizen-report-platform.html); [NSTDA: Traffy Fondue platform to enhance efficiency of public services](https://www.nstda.or.th/en/news/news-years-2022/traffy-fondue-platform-to-enhance-efficiency-of-public-services.html) | 2026 page (no date on the page; figures for 2023 and 2026); 2022 page created 26 September 2022 | Developed by the National Electronics and Computer Technology Center (NECTEC) under NSTDA. |
| (Evidence timeline) It won a Thai government award for public service innovation in September 2022; a prize shows officials valued it, not how many problems were fixed. | [NSTDA: NSTDA wins Public Sector Excellence Awards](https://www.nstda.or.th/en/news/news-years-2022/nstda-wins-public-sector-excellence-awards.html) | Ceremony 12 September 2022; article 14 September 2022 | The Office of the Public Sector Development Commission's Public Sector Excellence Awards, presented online by Deputy Prime Minister Wissanu Krea-ngam: NSTDA won in the public service category for Traffy Fondue, "a municipality complaint reporting and management system powered by artificial intelligence", developed by NECTEC's Intelligent Transport System research team under Dr Wasan Pattara-atikom. The "not how many problems were fixed" qualification is the lesson's (brief, section 8). |
| In May 2022 Bangkok's city government began to use it for reports from residents. | [Hansen and Dahiya, "Traffy Fondue: a smart city citizen engagement", Frontiers in Sustainable Cities 7](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsc.2025.1491621/full) | Published 2 April 2025 (online 12 March 2025); Thammasat University | The Bangkok Metropolitan Administration launched it on 31 May 2022, soon after Governor Chadchart Sittipunt took office. |
| Many other towns, offices and agencies across Thailand have joined; in September 2022 an agreement let local offices use it free for three years; by 2026 NSTDA says it is used in all 77 provinces by more than 34,000 agencies. | NSTDA 2022 and 2026 pages, as above | 23 September 2022 (MOU, 1 October 2022 to 30 September 2025); 2026 | 2022: an MOU with the Prime Minister's Office and the Department of Local Administration lets local administrative offices use it free for three years; 1,754 organisations then. 2026: active in all 77 provinces; 34,786 participating agencies (from 1,900 in 2023). The makers' own figures. |
| A resident uses LINE, a messaging app, and sends a photo, a few words and the place; the place is added from the phone's location. | Hansen and Dahiya; NSTDA 2022 and 2026 pages | 2025; 2022; 2026 | A LINE chatbot guides residents to share their location, choose a category, add a photo and a description (Hansen and Dahiya); photo and automatic GPS geotagging (NSTDA 2026); photo and GPS location (NSTDA 2022). |
| AI reads the report, sorts it by type (roads, flooding, lights) and sends it to the office responsible; this is the AI's part. | Hansen and Dahiya; NSTDA 2026 page; [Pattaya Mail, 18 July 2024, on NSTDA's AI upgrade](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/upgraded-ai-technology-in-traffy-fondue-to-accurately-understand-issues-and-respond-to-publics-needs-465956) | 2025; 2026; July 2024 | Hansen and Dahiya: an AI engine sorts complaints into categories, and a workflow engine assigns each to a task and pushes it to the responsible agency. NSTDA 2026: AI automatically classifies the issue and the report is routed to the responsible department. NSTDA's July 2024 statement (via Pattaya Mail) adds AI summaries, categorising from text and images, prioritising, suggesting solutions and filtering rude language; the lesson keeps to sorting and routing, which every source describes. Residents also pick a broad category themselves. |
| People take over: officials check and decide; some jobs need only a repair team, a bigger job needs money, a plan and more time; people and agencies do the fixing. | Hansen and Dahiya; NSTDA 2022 page | 2025; 2022 | Some complaints go to repair teams or contractors; others go through evaluation, budgeting and project execution (Hansen and Dahiya). The concerned departments resolve reports and post updates the reporter can see (NSTDA 2022). |
| The resident can follow the report and rate the result (evidence card). | NSTDA 2022 and 2026 pages | 2022; 2026 | Reporters can view updates; status is tracked until resolution; agencies are scored partly on citizen satisfaction ratings. |
| Bangkok says it received 588,842 reports in two years and resolved about 79%. | [Pattaya Mail: Bangkok's Traffy Fondue online platform resolves 79% of complaints in two years](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/bangkoks-traffy-fondue-online-platform-resolves-79-of-complaints-in-two-years-464385) | 29–30 June 2024 | Reports Governor Chadchart's two-year figures: 588,842 complaints, about 79% resolved; roads the largest category (201,355). "Resolved" is not defined, and the figures are the city's own. |
| "Resolved" means an official closed the case; in 2025 Bangkok's governor told officials not to close cases with photos that had nothing to do with the problem; so a closed case is not always a fixed problem. | [The Nation: Nearly 1 million Traffy Fondue reports reflect public trust, says Chadchart](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40052146) | Remarks 3 July 2025, published 4 July 2025 | The governor warned against "superficial resolutions", such as closing cases with unrelated photos, and told district directors to check that submitted images are genuine. "An official closed the case" follows from this: the responding office posts the result. The sentence "a closed case is not always a fixed problem" is the lesson's inference from the warning, not a measured rate. |
| In 2025 researchers at Thammasat University studied 98,000 reports and talked with 12 people who knew the system; it helped the city see where problems were and plan repairs; it needs a smartphone and the internet. | Hansen and Dahiya, as above | 2025 | Mixed methods: 98,000 reports from the platform's API (May to December 2022) and 12 semi-structured interviews with experts, practitioners and a BMA representative. The authors say it enabled broad data collection on urban issues and better problem-solving (BMA staff said more than 200 repair or infrastructure projects were identified in June 2022), and note that being digital-first is a challenge for people without smartphones or internet. Its abstract's 84% satisfaction and US$2.14 million yearly savings are in teacher notes only. |
| (Teacher notes) NSTDA's 2026 figures: over 2 million resolved tickets; average time to resolve down from 90 days (2023) to 15 days (2026). | NSTDA 2026 page | 2026 | As stated, the makers' own figures; how "resolved" is measured isn't explained. |

**Not confirmed in this revision:** NECTEC's own Thai-language account of the award (the page the brief names, [nectec.or.th, traffy-fondue-award-22](https://www.nectec.or.th/news/news-pr-news/traffy-fondue-award-22.html)) could not be opened through the page reader, so the lesson uses NSTDA's English account of the same award instead; NSTDA's 2026 page has no date of its own; the name's origin (said to join "traffic" with a Thai word for reporting) was not checked in a primary source, so the lesson doesn't explain it; the Thammasat paper's limitations section wasn't reached; no independent check of repair quality or of the resolution rate was found. The Code.org video is unchanged; its summary was written from DCMP's description, not from watching it.
