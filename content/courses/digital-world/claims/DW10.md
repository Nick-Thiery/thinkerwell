# DW10 AI in your community — claim record

Teacher-facing; not shown to learners. Checked 1 November 2026. The Sunvale tools (translation, leaf tool, eye photo helper), their weaknesses and the sort sentences are made up and labelled. SELENA+ is real and appears only in the reading and teacher notes, never in the fictional evidence or activity, and none of Sunvale's weaknesses are claimed for it.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| People with diabetes need regular eye checks, because the illness can harm their eyes. | [Ting and others, JAMA 2017 (abstract, Duke Scholars record)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1293398); [SNEC AI and digital health page](https://www.snec.com.sg/research-innovation/research-groups-platforms/research-groups/AI-digital-health) | JAMA 318(22):2211–23, December 2017, doi 10.1001/jama.2017.18152 | The study is about screening people with diabetes for diabetic retinopathy and related eye diseases; SNEC names DR, glaucoma and age-related macular degeneration as major blinding conditions. General background, not a statistic. |
| Researchers in Singapore built SELENA+, which looks at photos of the back of the eye and marks the ones an eye specialist should see. | SNEC page; JAMA abstract, as above | SNEC page undated (footer 2026) | SELENA+ (Singapore Eye Lesion Analyzer Plus) is a deep-learning system from SERI/SNEC's AI and digital health group and collaborators, trained to detect referable diabetic retinopathy, possible glaucoma and AMD from retinal photos. |
| In 2017 they reported tests with photos from Singapore's eye checks and from people in other countries. | JAMA abstract; SNEC page | 2017 | About 494,661 retinal images in all; primary validation on 14,880 patients from Singapore's national diabetic retinopathy screening programme, plus 10 external multi-ethnic datasets (different cameras, ethnic groups, ages); SNEC describes it as a multi-country study. |
| It found most of the problems that trained checkers found. | JAMA abstract | 2017 | Measured against professional graders, AUC 0.936 for referable DR (0.958 vision-threatening DR, 0.942 possible glaucoma, 0.931 AMD); the authors say it had "high sensitivity and specificity". "Most" is a plain-words version of high sensitivity. |
| The researchers said it still needed testing in real clinics. | JAMA abstract | 2017 | The authors say further research is needed to evaluate its use in health care settings. |
| (Teacher notes) approved by Singapore's health products regulator and CE-marked. | SNEC page | undated | The page says it is patented, approved by "Singapore Health Service Authority" (probably the Health Sciences Authority) and CE-marked. |
| (Teacher notes only, not learner text) The developers describe deploying it in Singapore's national screening programme. | [Gunasekeran and others, "National use of artificial intelligence for eye screening in Singapore", NEJM AI 1(12), SMU record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/9566) | Published online 19 November 2024 | The developers' own case study: SELENA+ deployed into the Singapore Integrated Diabetic Retinopathy Programme with regulatory approval; in a pilot of 1,712 patients it was less sensitive and less specific than human graders for referable DR (94.7%/82.2% against 98.9%/97.2%) and more sensitive for vision-threatening DR. Not an independent evaluation, and it doesn't report patient outcomes; the lesson makes no rollout or outcome claim. |
| A tool trained on data from other places isn't poor because of that, but must be tested with the people it will serve; health tools trained mostly on data from some places may not work as well elsewhere. | [WHO: first global report on AI in health](https://who.int/news/item/28-06-2021-who-issues-first-global-report-on-ai-in-health-and-six-guiding-principles-for-its-design-and-use); JAMA abstract (testing on several populations) | WHO 28 June 2021 | WHO warns that systems trained mainly on data from high-income countries may not perform well elsewhere (kept from the first draft; not reopened). The SELENA+ study illustrates testing across populations. |
| Translation tools make more mistakes in languages with little writing online. | [Stanford Report: How AI is leaving non-English speakers behind](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/05/digital-divide-ai-llms-exclusion-non-english-speakers-research) | May 2025 (kept; not reopened) | Language models perform worse in languages with little digital text. |
| Real offline phone tools for crop disease exist (teacher notes). | [IITA: Nuru cassava assistant](https://iita.org/news-item/african-farmers-get-new-help-against-cassava-diseases-nuru-their-artificially-intelligent-assistant/) | 2018 (kept; not reopened) | IITA and Penn State's offline phone app for cassava diseases. |
| Human checking of language technology (teacher notes). | [CLEAR Global: Voices that matter, skills that save](https://clearglobal.org/?p=75617) | kept from first draft; dropped from `sources` to stay within the limit | CLEAR Global's work combining language technology with human checking. |

**Not confirmed in this revision:** the full JAMA article (the journal page was blocked; the abstract came from a university record), so the exact sensitivity and specificity figures are not quoted to learners; whether SELENA+ is in routine use today beyond the developers' 2024 account; SNEC's "Health Service Authority" wording.
