# DW13 Checking photos, videos and voices: claim record

Teacher-facing. Not shown to learners. Every Greenhill post is made up and labelled as fiction (four posts since 8 November 2026). Checked 8 November 2026 unless a row says otherwise.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Many people use AI tools openly for art, film effects and lessons, and say that AI helped; that is not the same as a fake | Teaching point (definition); no study cited | — | A distinction the brief asks for (synthetic creativity against deception), illustrated only with the made-up comedy club's labelled goat. |
| AI-made pictures often show artifacts such as extra or missing fingers, distorted text and shadows that don't match | [Kamali and others, CHI '25](https://www.hullmanlab.northwestern.edu/assets/papers/2025-photorealism.pdf); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11989v1) | CHI 2025 (ACM), DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713962; re-opened 8 November 2026 | A taxonomy of artifacts in images from Midjourney, Stable Diffusion and Firefly, including "hands with extra or missing fingers" and "distorted or unconventional glyphs". |
| These details are clues, not proof; real photos can look strange too | Kamali and others (above) | Re-opened 8 November 2026 | Section 4: physics distortions "can also occur in real photographs, for example as seen with fish-eye lens distortions". |
| In a 2025 study by researchers in the United States, more than 50,000 people judged real photos and AI pictures online; they wrongly called about one in four real photos AI-made, and missed about one in four AI pictures | Kamali and others (above) | Re-opened 8 November 2026 | Northwestern University researchers; 50,444 participants, 749,828 observations, 450 AI-made and 149 real images. Section 5.1: "participants correctly identified AI-generated images and authentic photographs in 76% and 74% of observations". So about 26% of judgments on real photos and 24% on AI images were wrong. One online study with the authors' own image set, not a general rate. Participants' countries are not stated, so the lesson says the researchers, not the participants, were in the United States. |
| Newer tools make fewer mistakes; it is becoming very hard to tell by looking | [News Literacy Project, Scroll smarter](https://newslit.org/news-and-research/scroll-smarter-ai-frustrations/) | 10 March 2026; read 1 November 2026, not re-opened on 8 November | Says "it's becoming nearly impossible to tell what's real and what's AI just by looking", and recommends checking the source, context and a reverse image search. |
| Labels can get lost when a picture is reshared | News Literacy Project (above) | Same | "Fabricated images are sometimes reposted without AI labels, even if the original had one." |
| Check where a post first came from; agreeing sources may all have copied one post | Teaching point shared with Lesson 14 (lateral reading; see its claim record) | — | Brief, section 11: "several agreeing websites may copy the same source". |
| Fact-checking groups do this work for pictures that spread widely; Mafindo in Indonesia (teacher note) | [Liputan6 Cek Fakta](https://www.liputan6.com/cek-fakta/read/6293913/hoaks-masih-beredar-luas-mafindo-masyarakat-perlu-tingkatkan-literasi-digital) | 2026; kept from the first draft, not re-opened | Indonesian news report quoting Mafindo. The reading's general sentence names no group. |
| Deepfakes of real voices are used to trick people for money (sensitive note) | [US FTC, family emergency scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/scams/family-emergency-scams) | Kept from the first draft; not re-opened | The FTC's consumer page on scams that use a cloned voice of a relative. |
| The video's content (clues, tools not always right) | [PBS NewsHour Classroom lesson plan](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/classroom/lesson-plans/2025/04/recognizing-ai-generated-content) | April 2025; not re-opened | The basis of the Watch summary, kept unchanged. The video itself has not been watched. Its content note now says the lesson treats visual clues as reasons to check, never proof. |

Not confirmed: the News Literacy Project's "Scroll smarter" page, the FTC page, the PBS lesson plan and the Liputan6 report were not re-opened on 8 November 2026 (a related News Literacy Project page from September 2025 was opened, but it gives no checking advice and isn't cited).
