# DW13 Spotting fake photos, videos and voices: claim record

Teacher-facing. Not shown to learners. Every Greenhill post is made up and labelled as fiction.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI-made pictures often show artifacts such as extra or missing fingers, distorted text and shadows that don't match | [Kamali and others, CHI '25](https://www.hullmanlab.northwestern.edu/assets/papers/2025-photorealism.pdf) | CHI 2025 (ACM), DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713962 | A taxonomy of artifacts in images from Midjourney, Stable Diffusion and Firefly, including "hands with extra or missing fingers" and "distorted or unconventional glyphs". |
| These details are clues, not proof; real photos can look strange too | Kamali and others (above) | CHI 2025 | Artifacts vary in visibility and are not definitive; physics violations "can also occur in real photographs" (for example a fish-eye lens). |
| In a 2025 study, people wrongly labelled about one in four real photographs as AI-made | Kamali and others (above) | CHI 2025 | Participants labelled real photographs correctly 74% of the time (image-level mean), so about 26% were called AI-made. One online study with the authors' image set, not a general rate. |
| Newer tools make fewer mistakes; it is becoming very hard to tell by looking | [News Literacy Project, Scroll smarter](https://newslit.org/news-and-research/scroll-smarter-ai-frustrations/) | 10 March 2026 | Says "it's becoming nearly impossible to tell what's real and what's AI just by looking", and recommends checking the source, context and a reverse image search. |
| Labels can be lost when a picture is reposted (teacher note) | News Literacy Project (above) | 10 March 2026 | "Fabricated images are sometimes reposted without AI labels, even if the original had one." |
| Deepfakes of real voices are used to trick people for money (teacher note) | [US FTC, family emergency scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/scams/family-emergency-scams) | Kept from the first draft; not re-opened on 1 November 2026 | The FTC's consumer page on scams that use a cloned voice of a relative. |
| The video's content (clues, tools not always right) | [PBS NewsHour Classroom lesson plan](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/classroom/lesson-plans/2025/04/recognizing-ai-generated-content) | April 2025; not re-opened on 1 November 2026 (the request was blocked) | The basis of the Watch summary, kept unchanged. The video itself has not been watched. |
| Mafindo checks viral pictures and says AI hoaxes are harder to tell apart (teacher note) | [Liputan6 Cek Fakta](https://www.liputan6.com/cek-fakta/read/6293913/hoaks-masih-beredar-luas-mafindo-masyarakat-perlu-tingkatkan-literasi-digital) | 2026; kept from the first draft, not re-opened | Indonesian news report quoting Mafindo. |

Not confirmed on 1 November 2026: the News Literacy Project's 2025 "AI or not?" lesson plan and the MIT Media Lab's Detect Fakes page were in the first draft's sources; the first could not be opened and was replaced; the second was opened (it says "there's no single tell-tale sign") but dropped to keep the list short.
