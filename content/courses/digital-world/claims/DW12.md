# DW12 Cameras that know faces — claim record

Teacher-facing; not shown to learners. Checked 1 November 2026. Greenport (Rosa's phone, the fair, the street camera plan, the test with 12 wrong matches in 1,000) and the sort sentences are made up and labelled.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facial recognition finds a face, measures details, turns them into numbers, compares them with stored faces and gives a similarity score; a match is a best guess, not proof. | [NIST IR 8280, FRVT Part 3: Demographic effects](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2019/NIST.IR.8280.pdf); [EFF Street Level Surveillance: Face recognition](https://sls.eff.org/technologies/face-recognition) | NIST December 2019; EFF page undated (both kept from the first draft; not reopened) | Algorithms compare face templates and return similarity scores against a threshold; one-to-one verification and one-to-many search are distinct; errors (false matches and false non-matches) occur. |
| A phone that opens for your face usually keeps only numbers from your face, not a photo. | [Apple Platform Security: Facial matching security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/sece151358d1/web) | Apple's current guide (opened 1 November 2026) | Face ID saves "mathematical representations" of the face calculated at enrolment, discards face images captured during use, keeps the data encrypted in the Secure Enclave, and it never leaves the device. This is one maker's design; "usually" in the lesson allows for others. |
| Helpful uses: opening a phone, speeding up boarding at some airports, helping search for a missing person. | EFF page; WIRED video transcript (as summarised in the Watch note) | kept from the first draft | Common documented uses. |
| In 2019 a US government science agency tested nearly 200 face recognition tools; many wrongly matched people far more often for some groups (women, older people, children, people of some backgrounds); the best made far fewer mistakes. | NIST IR 8280, as above | December 2019 | 189 mostly commercial algorithms from 99 developers; false matches often 10 to over 100 times more frequent for some demographic groups, varying by algorithm; the most accurate algorithms showed smaller differences. |
| Cameras can recognise people from far away without them knowing, and track where they go. | EFF page, as above | kept | Face recognition can be applied to footage from public cameras to identify and follow people. |
| In 2019 San Francisco became the first US city to ban facial recognition by its police and other city offices. | [Al Jazeera, 15 May 2019](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/05/san-francisco-city-ban-facial-recognition-police-190515055326111.html) | May 2019 (kept; not reopened) | San Francisco's supervisors banned use by police and other city departments. |
| Since 2025 an EU law has banned some uses: police may not use live facial recognition in public places except in a few serious cases, such as a search for a missing person, and a judge or independent official must agree first. | [AI Act Article 5 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Commission AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5); [European Commission: AI Act overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); also [European Parliament press release, 13 March 2024](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20240308IPR19015/artificial-intelligence-act-meps-adopt-landmark-law) and [Bundesnetzagentur on prohibited practices](https://bundesnetzagentur.de/EN/Areas/Digitalisation/AI/08_ProhibitedPractices/start.html) | Prohibitions apply from 2 February 2025 | Art. 5(1)(h) bans real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement unless strictly necessary for: a targeted search for victims of abduction or trafficking, or missing persons; preventing a specific, substantial and imminent threat to life or a terrorist attack; or locating or identifying suspects of listed offences punishable by at least four years. Art. 5(3): each use needs prior authorisation by a judicial authority or independent administrative authority (urgent use may start if authorisation is asked within 24 hours). Art. 5(5): only where a member state's national law provides for it. Other (for example after-the-fact) remote biometric identification is not prohibited but is high-risk; the Commission's page gives 2 December 2027 for those obligations (as of November 2026). |
| The law also bans copying face photos in bulk from the internet or security cameras to build a face recognition database. | Article 5 and Commission overview, as above | 2 February 2025 | Art. 5(1)(e) bans AI systems that "create or expand facial recognition databases through the untargeted scraping of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage". It is limited to untargeted scraping for facial recognition databases; it does not ban every use of a face photo found online. |
| Ordinary cameras are not banned, and other places allow much wider use. | Article 5 (bans particular uses and purposes, not cameras); general | — | The prohibitions concern specific AI practices; recording cameras without these uses are outside them. "Other places allow much wider use" is a general statement, not tied to a named country. |

**Not confirmed in this revision:** the first draft's NIST, EFF and San Francisco sources were kept without being reopened; how other phone makers store face data was not checked (hence "usually"); the date for high-risk obligations has moved before and should be checked again before teaching.
