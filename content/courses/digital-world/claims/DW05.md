| In January 2023, a news magazine reported on about three dozen workers in Kenya who labelled harmful text for a well-known AI tool in 2021 and 2022 | [TIME, Billy Perrigo](https://time.com/6247678/openai-chatgpt-kenya-workers/), read as the [Portside reprint](https://portside.org/node/30453/printable/print) | 18 January 2023 | About three dozen Sama workers in Nairobi; labelling began November 2021; Sama ended the work in February 2022 and delivered its last batch in March 2022. |
| They took home about 1.32 to 2 US dollars an hour; the AI company paid the firm 12.50 dollars an hour; the firm said this also paid offices, managers and other costs | TIME (above) | 18 January 2023 | Take-home $1.32–$2 depending on seniority and performance; contract rate $12.50 an hour to Sama, which said it “covers all costs, like infrastructure expenses, and salary and benefits”. Sama said workers earned $1.46–$3.74 after tax; TIME's sources disputed that. |
| In August 2023, a newspaper spoke to 36 online workers in the Philippines; all but two said some pay came late, was cut or never came; the platform said this was rare | [The Washington Post, Rebecca Tan and Regine Cabato](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2023/08/28/scale-ai-remotasks-philippines-artificial-intelligence/), read as the [Stars and Stripes reprint](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/asia_pacific/2023-08-29/ai-boom-overseas-workers-11208813.html) | August 2023 (URL 28 August; reprint says 30 August) | 34 of 36 freelancers reported payments “delayed, reduced or canceled”; Scale AI said delays were “exceedingly rare”. Informal government estimates: over 2 million Filipino crowdworkers. |
| In the 1800s, people in England called very low-paid, hard work “sweating”, and later spoke of a sweatshop | [Britannica: Sweatshop](https://www.britannica.com/topic/sweatshop) | Undated encyclopedia entry | “Sweater” in use by 1850 for an employer demanding tedious work for very low pay; “sweating” widespread in the 1880s. The first use of “sweatshop” was not confirmed. |
| In 1909, a British law set a minimum wage in four trades, such as tailoring and making chains | [Modern Records Centre, University of Warwick: Trade Boards](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/library/mrc/collections/digital/tradeboard/boards/) | Archive page, undated | The Trade Boards Act 1909 introduced a legally enforceable minimum wage in four “sweated” industries: chainmaking, lace finishing, paper box making and ready-made and wholesale bespoke tailoring. “Workers and reformers pushed for change” is a general description; the page does not detail the campaign. |
| In 2023, researchers at the University of Oxford checked 15 online work platforms (some selling AI data work) on pay, conditions, contracts, management and a voice for workers; none met their basic standards; the best scored 5 of 10 | [Fairwork Cloudwork Ratings 2023](https://fairwork.oii.ox.ac.uk/en/fw/publications/fairwork-cloudwork-ratings-2023-work-in-the-planetary-labour-market/); [OII news release](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/new-oxford-report-sheds-light-on-labour-malpractices-in-the-remote-work-and-ai-booms/) | 20 July 2023 | 15 web-based platforms, five principles; three platforms scored 5/10 (Comeup, Prolific, Terawork), four scored 0; no platform met minimum standards. Microwork platforms serving AI companies were among the lowest scores. |
| They asked 250 people who do small online tasks; on average these workers earned about 2.15 US dollars an hour; more than a quarter of their time was unpaid, such as looking for tasks | [Fairwork report PDF](https://fair.work/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2023/07/Fairwork-Cloudwork-Ratings-2023-Red.pdf), p. 19 and p. 10 | July 2023 | Survey February–May 2023, 752 workers in 94 countries, “do not aim to build a representative set of experiences”. 250 microworkers (51 countries): average $50.30 a week, given as about $2.15 an hour; 26.85% of platform time unpaid (looking for jobs 38.4%, unpaid qualification tests 28%, applying 12.4%). The report does not say outright whether the hourly figure counts unpaid time (the numbers suggest it does), so the lesson gives it without that detail. |
| After the study, some platforms changed their rules, for example by setting a minimum wage | OII news release (above) | 20 July 2023 | Comeup and Terawork introduced minimum-wage policies; four platforms made 17 changes in 12 months. |
| Fairwork links platform work to older piece-rate work | Fairwork report PDF, p. 28 | July 2023 | “Precarious piece-rate work has a long legacy and is not especially innovative or historically distinct.” Teacher notes only. |
| Quick check: workers got far less than the firm in the middle was paid | TIME (above) | 18 January 2023 | Supported, with the qualification (in the feedback) that the $12.50 also covered the firm's other costs. |
