# DW16 Who makes the rules for AI?: claim record

Teacher-facing. Not shown to learners. Hillford, its meeting and its speakers are made up and labelled as fiction. What a learner recommends is their own view and is never checked against this table. Checked 8 November 2026 unless a row says otherwise.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A government or parliament makes laws everyone must follow; courts can act when a law is broken; companies write rules for their own tools; residents can take part when leaders ask | General civics, illustrated by the made-up Hillford meeting | — | Not a sourced claim about any one country. |
| In 2021, all 193 member states of UNESCO adopted a guideline (recommendation) on the ethics of AI | [NZ National Commission for UNESCO](https://unesco.org.nz/knowledge-hub/ethics-of-artificial-intelligence-recommendation) | Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, 2021; read 1 November 2026, not re-opened | "The first-ever global standard on AI ethics", "adopted by all 193 Member States". A recommendation, not a treaty. |
| In February 2024, ASEAN's digital ministers shared the ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics | [ASEAN Guide (PDF)](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ASEAN-Guide-on-AI-Governance-and-Ethics_beautified_201223_v2.pdf); [Rajah & Tann client update, March 2024](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2024-03_ASEAN-Guide-AI-Governance-Ethics-v2.pdf) | Unveiled at the 4th ASEAN Digital Ministers' Meeting, 2 February 2024; both opened 8 November 2026 | The date and meeting come from the law firm's summary; the PDF is the guide itself. |
| It sets out seven ideas, such as fairness, privacy, safety and putting people first | ASEAN Guide (above) | Same | Seven principles: transparency and explainability, fairness and equity, security and safety, human-centricity, privacy and data governance, accountability and integrity, robustness and reliability. |
| It is voluntary, and each country keeps its own laws | ASEAN Guide (above) | Same | Adoption is "voluntary"; it does not override existing laws, and developers and deployers must still follow national and sector laws; "a living document that should be periodically reviewed". |
| It notes that its members are at different stages with digital technology | ASEAN Guide (above) | Same | Organisations should account for "the differing levels of digital maturity across ASEAN". |
| The ASEAN guide says one of its aims is to build trust, so that people and groups can use AI with care | ASEAN Guide; Rajah & Tann (above) | Same | Objectives include helping organisations and governments "use AI responsibly and build user trust"; the summary: build consumer confidence and ease cross-border deployment. National recommendations include raising public awareness of AI's risks and benefits (teacher note). |
| The EU's AI Act came into force in August 2024, with parts starting on different dates | [AI Act Service Desk, implementation timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/timeline/timeline-implementation-eu-ai-act) | Opened 8 November 2026 | Entry into force 1 August 2024; prohibitions and AI literacy from 2 February 2025; general-purpose AI obligations from 2 August 2025; transparency rules (Article 50) from 2 August 2026; after the Digital Omnibus, new prohibitions on non-consensual sexual deepfakes and child sexual abuse material from 2 December 2026, Annex III high-risk rules from 2 December 2027 and Annex I (regulated products) from 2 August 2028. |
| It sorts uses by risk; since February 2025 some uses are banned, such as scores that treat people unfairly | [European Commission, AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); [AI Act Service Desk, Article 5](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5); timeline (above) | Commission page and Article 5 read 1 November 2026; date confirmed 8 November | Social scoring is banned where it leads to detrimental treatment in unrelated contexts or treatment unjustified or disproportionate to the behaviour. Emotion recognition at work and school (except medical or safety reasons) and police live face identification in public (three narrow exceptions) are in the teacher note. |
| Since August 2026, a chatbot must make clear that it is a machine | Commission page (above); timeline (above) | Article 50 from 2 August 2026 | People should be told they are dealing with a machine unless it is obvious; deepfakes must be labelled. |
| Strict checks for high-risk uses, such as marking exams, start in December 2027 | Commission page (read 1 November); timeline (8 November) | After the Digital Omnibus | "Exam scoring in education" is listed as high risk (Commission page); Annex III rules apply from 2 December 2027 (timeline). |
| Most uses, like spam filters, have no special AI rules, but other laws, like privacy laws, still apply | Commission page (above); [AI Act Service Desk, Article 2](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2) | Article 2(7) and 2(9); read 1 November 2026 | Minimal-risk uses get no AI Act rules; the Act "shall not affect" the GDPR and is "without prejudice" to EU consumer protection and product safety law. The Article 2 page left the lesson's sources on 8 November (to stay at five) and is kept here. |
| In Thailand, Traffy Fondue, a public service built by a national science agency, uses AI to sort residents' reports and send each to the right office, where people decide what to do | [NSTDA via ThaiPR.net](https://www.thaipr.net/?p=3497519); [Hansen and Dahiya, Frontiers in Sustainable Cities](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsc.2025.1491621/full) | NSTDA release 17 July 2024; paper 2 April 2025; read 1 November 2026, not re-opened | Developed by NECTEC-NSTDA; an "AI engine" sorts complaints and a workflow engine pushes each to the responsible agency; officers and agencies do the work. NSTDA reports more than 940,000 reports, June 2022 to July 2024 (its own figures, teacher note only). |
| Rules about privacy and about who checks the AI's work are meant to help people trust tools like this | Reasoning from the ASEAN guide's stated aim (build trust) and its principles (privacy, accountability) | — | A teaching connection, not a finding that such rules raised trust in Traffy Fondue. |
| Indonesia and Malaysia have national AI ethics guidelines, not laws (teacher note) | [Kominfo Surat Edaran No. 9 of 2023](https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/view/id/883/t/surat+edaran+menteri+komunikasi+dan+informatika+nomor+9+tahun+2023); [MyGOV Malaysia, National Guidelines on AI Governance and Ethics](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/my-initiative/whole-government-digital-services/tadbir-urus/national-guidelines-on-artificial-intelligence-governance-and-ethics-aige) | 19 December 2023; September 2024 | Kept from the first draft, not re-opened.
