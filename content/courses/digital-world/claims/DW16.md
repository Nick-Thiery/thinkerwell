# DW16 Who makes the rules for AI?: claim record

Teacher-facing. Not shown to learners. Hillford, its meeting and its council's ideas are made up and labelled as fiction. What a learner recommends is their own view and is never checked against this table.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| In 2021, all 193 UNESCO member states adopted the first global agreement on the ethics of AI; it says AI should protect human rights and stay under human control | [NZ National Commission for UNESCO](https://unesco.org.nz/knowledge-hub/ethics-of-artificial-intelligence-recommendation) | Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, 2021 | "The first-ever global standard on AI ethics", "adopted by all 193 Member States"; human rights and dignity are its "cornerstone"; it stresses human oversight. A recommendation, not a treaty (the lesson calls it a guideline). |
| In 2024, ASEAN countries shared a guide for AI with ideas such as fairness, privacy and safety; it is a guideline | [ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics](https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ASEAN-Guide-on-AI-Governance-and-Ethics_beautified_201223_v2.pdf); [Asia Pacific Foundation of Canada summary](https://asiapacific.ca/publication/asean-issues-guidelines-artificial-intelligence) | Released 2 February 2024 at the 4th ASEAN Digital Ministers' Meeting | Seven principles (transparency, fairness, security, reliability, human-centricity, privacy, accountability); non-binding and "entirely voluntary". The ASEAN PDF itself was not re-opened on 1 November 2026 (the request was blocked); the summary was. |
| The EU's AI Act came into force in August 2024, with parts starting on different dates | [European Commission, AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) | Page read 1 November 2026, after the AI Omnibus (in force 27 July 2026) | In force 1 August 2024; prohibitions 1–8 from February 2025 (a ninth, on non-consensual sexual content, from December 2026); transparency rules from August 2026; high-risk rules for Annex III uses from 2 December 2027 and for AI in regulated products from 2 August 2028. |
| It sorts uses by risk; banned uses include scores that treat people unfairly and guessing students' feelings at school (activity: except for medical or safety reasons) | Commission page (above); [AI Act Service Desk, Article 5](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5) | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 5 | Social scoring is banned where it leads to detrimental treatment in unrelated contexts or treatment unjustified or disproportionate to the behaviour; emotion recognition in workplaces and education is banned except for "medical or safety reasons". |
| Police may not identify faces live in public places, except in a few serious cases such as finding a missing person (activity) | Article 5 (above) | Same | Real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement is banned unless strictly necessary for searching for victims of abduction or trafficking and missing persons, preventing a specific imminent threat or terrorist attack, or finding suspects of offences with at least four years' maximum sentence. |
| Marking exams is high risk and needs strict checks; its rules start in December 2027 (activity) | Commission page (above) | After the AI Omnibus | "Exam scoring in education" is listed as high risk; Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027 (originally 2 August 2026). |
| Many health tools count as high risk (activity) | Commission page (above) | Same | The page lists AI safety components in robot-assisted surgery as high risk, and AI in regulated products (which include medical devices) as high risk from August 2028. "Many", not all; a clinic triage tool's exact category was not checked. |
| A chatbot must tell people it is a machine (activity) | Commission page (above) | Transparency rules from August 2026 | People should be told they are dealing with a machine; deepfakes must be labelled. |
| Spam filters and games have no special AI rules, but other laws, like privacy laws, still apply | Commission page (above); [AI Act Service Desk, Article 2](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2) | Article 2(7) and 2(9) | Minimal-risk uses get no AI Act rules; the Act "shall not affect" the GDPR and is "without prejudice" to EU consumer protection and product safety law. |
| In Thailand, people report problems such as broken roads, floods or street lights on Traffy Fondue, a public platform built by a national science agency | [NSTDA via ThaiPR.net](https://www.thaipr.net/?p=3497519); [Hansen and Dahiya, Frontiers in Sustainable Cities](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frsc.2025.1491621/full); [NSTDA, 2019](https://www.nstda.or.th/en/news/news-years-2019/ministry-of-interior-to-employ-nstda’s-smart-city-technology-in-its-city-management.html) | NSTDA release 17 July 2024; paper 2 April 2025; NSTDA news 17 September 2019 | Developed by NECTEC-NSTDA; Bangkok launched it on 31 May 2022; residents report through a LINE chatbot with location, category, photo and description. In Bangkok in 2022 the most reported types were road, flood and light. |
| Its AI sorts each report and sends it to the right office, where people still decide and fix it | NSTDA release; Hansen and Dahiya (above) | July 2024; 2025 | The paper: an "AI engine" sorts complaints into categories and a workflow engine pushes each to the responsible agency. NSTDA (2024): the system analyses the problem type automatically and notifies the responsible agency; it now also summarises, ranks by urgency, suggests fixes and filters rude words. Officers and agencies do the work. |
| Its makers say it received over 940,000 reports in two years; a count shows use, not how well problems were solved | NSTDA release (above) | June 2022 to July 2024 | More than 940,000 complaints; over 733,000 "completed" (77%); 77 provinces; more than 15,000 agencies. The operator's own figures; "completed" is a status in the system, not an independent check. The paper reports 84% citizen satisfaction and notes the platform is digital-only, which can leave out people without smartphones or internet. |
| Indonesia and Malaysia have national AI ethics guidelines, not laws (teacher note) | [Kominfo Surat Edaran No. 9 of 2023](https://jdih.komdigi.go.id/produk_hukum/view/id/883/t/surat+edaran+menteri+komunikasi+dan+informatika+nomor+9+tahun+2023); [MyGOV Malaysia, National Guidelines on AI Governance and Ethics](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/my-initiative/whole-government-digital-services/tadbir-urus/national-guidelines-on-artificial-intelligence-governance-and-ethics-aige) | 19 December 2023; September 2024 (MOSTI, seven principles) | Kept from the first draft, not re-opened on 1 November 2026. The Malaysian page was dropped from the lesson's sources to stay within six. |
