# Lesson 8 (DW08, `dw-when-ai-gets-it-wrong`): claim record

Teacher-facing; not shown to learners. Checked 1 November 2026. Riverbend Learning Centre, its three tools and the activity's results (19, 16 and 9 of 20) are made up and are not listed here.

| Claim | Source (link) | Date or version | What the source actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Many speech tools are much less accurate with children's voices | [Kid-Whisper, Attia and others (AIES 2024)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31618); read as [arXiv 2309.07927 v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07927) | AIES 2024; v3, 15 May 2024 | Whisper models without fine-tuning had word error rates 6–10 times adults' on scripted children's speech and 3–5 times on conversational children's speech. One tool family, specific test sets; “many speech tools” rests on the paper's review of earlier work (“the gap between children and adult ASR even in English”). |
| One reason is far fewer recordings of children to learn from | Kid-Whisper (above) | 2024 | Children's speech databases “are limited and difficult to collect and transcribe”; the paper also names children's more variable speech as a main cause, so “one reason” is accurate. |
| Missing examples are one possible cause of bias; others are wrong or unfair labels, the goal, design, and using a tool where it wasn't made for | [Suresh and Guttag, EAAMO 2021 (arXiv 1901.10002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.10002) | EAAMO '21, October 2021 (v5, 1 December 2021) | Seven sources of harm: historical, representation (missing or under-sampled groups), measurement (labels and proxies), aggregation, learning (the objective), evaluation (tests) and deployment (use different from design). “Not all problems should be blamed on the data.” |
| A gap in results shows something is wrong, but not why | Suresh and Guttag (above) | 2021 | Their two examples have the same symptom (worse results for women) with different causes: missing data (fixed by more data) and biased human ratings used as labels (not fixed by more data). |
| More data doesn't fix every cause; if labels are unfair, more examples with the same labels won't help | Suresh and Guttag (above), section 4.2 | 2021 | Collecting more data that goes through the same measurement “will likely be ineffective” for measurement or historical bias. |
| The Riverbend translation tool's pattern (worse in languages with little writing online) is found in real AI language tools | [Stanford Report](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2025/05/digital-divide-ai-llms-exclusion-non-english-speakers-research) | 2025 | Major models work well in English, worse in Vietnamese and worst in Nahuatl; the main cause given is insufficient data. |
| Even when nobody meant to leave people out, the result can still be unfair | Suresh and Guttag (above) | 2021 | Harm can arise from data and design choices without intent (for example historical bias “even if data is perfectly measured and sampled”). The lesson no longer claims no real system was built to exclude anyone. |
| Testing with many groups and comparing results finds bias; collect more examples only with permission; a person should check important decisions | Suresh and Guttag (evaluation bias, reporting results by subgroup); [Code.org lesson plan](https://curriculum.code.org/csp-20/unit9/6/); [Experience AI Lesson 3](https://experience-ai.org/en/units/foundations-of-ai/lessons/3) | 2021; undated | Suresh and Guttag recommend reporting metrics on finer subgroups. Permission and human checking are the course's rules for good practice, not research findings. Code.org's and Experience AI's pages could not be reopened on 1 November 2026; they were kept from the earlier draft. |
| Glossary: training data is examples used to build a model; here each comes with its answer | Suresh and Guttag (section 2: training data, features and labels) | 2021 | Datasets are split into training and test data; labels are chosen for supervised tasks. Not all training data has labels (Lesson 7's writing does not), hence “Here”. |
