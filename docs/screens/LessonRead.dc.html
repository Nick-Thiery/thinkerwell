Lesson 10

Why do people build towns near rivers?

What makes one place better than another for a community to live?

Learning goal

Explain how rivers help communities and what problems they can bring.

About 30–50 min. Stop and come back any time.
Listen Key words

Look at the map. Where would you build a new town? Guess now. You can change your mind later.

Near the river On the hill In the forest
Every community needs water to drink, cook and wash. In many places, a river gives fresh water all year. Farmers dig small canals to carry river water to their fields. When a river floods, it leaves a layer of mud on the land, and this mud makes the soil fertile, so crops like wheat and rice grow well. Rivers also give people fish to eat. That is why some of the first large settlements grew beside rivers. In ancient Egypt, people farmed along the Nile, and in today's Iraq, people farmed between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers. The River site in Riverlands has the same kind of flat, fertile land.
