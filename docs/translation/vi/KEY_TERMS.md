# Vietnamese: key terms to decide first

Hidden preview, drafted by AI. These words come up again and again across the interface and the 24 lessons. Please have **one native speaker** decide them all before the lesson-by-lesson review, so the whole course uses the same word for the same idea. A change here is applied across the course at once (tell Nick).

- **Suggested** is what the draft uses now.
- **Other options** are real alternatives, not mistakes.
- Write the final choice in **Decision**.

Style used throughout: everyday Vietnamese for 10 to 17-year-olds. **bạn** for the learner ("you"); **mình** when the learner speaks ("Mình mới đến đây", self-check items "Mình ..."). No *vui lòng*, *làm ơn* or *xin* used as "please"; no *quý vị* or *quý khách* to learners; no exclamation marks; sentence case (Vietnamese capitalises only the first word and proper names). Pages for adults (the Educators pages, teacher guides, Settings, For organisations, the information sheet, the consent form, code cards) also say **bạn** to the reader, in a more careful register, and the consent form's reader is **phụ huynh hoặc người giám hộ** (parent or guardian). Whether adults would rather be addressed another way (*quý thầy cô*, *anh/chị*) is a question for the reviewers.

Numbers follow Vietnamese practice: 1.000 (a full stop for thousands), 2,5 (a comma for decimals). Dates: "ngày 5 tháng 9".

## Names

- People and places keep their names: Riverstone, Tallgrass Valley, Bayview, Riverlands and every person's name stay as they are.
- Real places use the usual Vietnamese name (Ai Cập, Trung Quốc, Ấn Độ, Campuchia, Nhật Bản, Hy Lạp, Lưỡng Hà ...). Where the usual Vietnamese name of a place is itself contested or political, do not choose a side: keep to the neutral name of the country or region and flag it.
- Lesson 4, faiths: Hồi giáo, Kitô giáo (also written Ki-tô giáo), Do Thái giáo, Ấn Độ giáo, Phật giáo; "Thượng đế" for God where the sentence covers several faiths; Thổ dân Úc for Aboriginal Australians. Please have someone check this lesson with particular care, ideally a person from each faith it names.
