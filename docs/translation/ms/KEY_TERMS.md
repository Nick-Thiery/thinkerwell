# Bahasa Melayu (Malaysian Malay): key terms to decide first

Drafted by AI and offered to everyone while native-speaker review is in progress, as Indonesian is (`docs/translation/ms/README.md`). These words come up again and again across the interface and the 24 lessons. Please have **one native Malaysian speaker** decide them all before the lesson-by-lesson review, so the whole course uses the same word for the same idea. A change here is applied across the course at once (tell Nick).

- **Suggested** is what the draft uses now.
- **Other options** are real alternatives, not mistakes.
- Write the final choice in **Decision**.

Style used throughout: standard Malaysian Malay (DBP spelling) as Malaysian schools use it, never Indonesian; plain and warm for 10 to 17-year-olds. **kamu** for the learner; **saya** when the learner speaks ("Saya baru di sini", self-check items "Saya ..."). Pages for adults (Settings, the Educators pages, teacher guides, answer keys, For organisations, the information sheet, the consent form, code cards, teachers' notes) say **anda**, in lower case as Malaysians write it, or leave "you" out. No *sila*, *silakan* or *tolong* (the English never says "please"); no exclamation marks; sentence case.

Numbers follow Malaysian practice, the same as English: 1,000 and 2.5. Dates: "5 September 2026".

## Names

- People and places keep their names: Riverstone, Tallgrass Valley, Bayview, Riverlands and every person's name stay as they are.
- Real places use the usual Malaysian spelling: Mesir, Eropah, Jepun, Itali, Sepanyol, Perancis, Kemboja, Mesopotamia; China, Iraq, Syria, Brazil as in English; Sungai Nil, Sungai Tigris, Sungai Euphrates.
- Lesson 4, faiths: Islam (orang Islam), Kristian, Yahudi, Hindu, Buddha; "Tuhan" for God; Orang Asli Australia for Aboriginal Australians. Please have someone check this lesson with particular care, ideally a person from each faith it names.
