# Brief: translating Thinkerwell into Malaysian Malay (Bahasa Melayu)

Thinkerwell is a free, offline-first learning website ("Social studies and beyond") with one course, "Exploring Our World": 24 social-studies lessons in 4 sections, each with five steps (Read, Write, Speak, Watch, Reflect), plus a short check at the end of each section. It is for refugee, displaced and under-served young people aged about 10 to 17, many learning to read, some reading slowly, on shared tablets with bad Wi-Fi. A Malaysian partner organisation will use the Malay version, so it must read as natural, warm, everyday **Malaysian Malay**.

## Language

- **Standard Malaysian Malay (Bahasa Melayu)** as used in Malaysian schools, with Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka (DBP) spelling. NOT Bahasa Indonesia. Translate from the English. Many everyday words differ from Indonesian, and an Indonesian word in the Malay is a mistake. ## Who you are speaking to (register)

- **Learners: "kamu"** (and "-mu" if natural, e.g. "jawapanmu"; "jawapan kamu" is also fine). Never "anda" or "engkau" to a learner.
- **Adults: "anda", always in lower case even mid-sentence**, as Malaysians write it: teachers, families and partner organisations (Settings, the Educators pages, teacher guides, answer keys, the setup checklist, the class view, For organisations, the information sheet, the consent form, code cards, and teachers' notes in a lesson). Never "kamu" to an adult. Where a sentence reads naturally without "you", leave it out.
- **The learner speaking: "saya"** ("Saya baru di sini"; self-check items start "Saya ..."; sentence starters in the learner's voice use "saya").
- No "sila", "silakan" or "tolong" (the English never says "please"). No exclamation marks. Sentence case (only the first word and names capitalised), like the English.
- Plain, warm, everyday Malay for 10 to 17-year-olds, some of whom read slowly. Not stiff, not like an official letter or a textbook. Short sentences. Kind about mistakes: never "salah" (wrong), "gagal" (failed) or similar.

## Rules

1. Say the same thing as the English, no more, no less. Don't add explanations, don't drop details.
2. Keep every `{placeholder}` exactly as it is (same name, same braces); you may move it within the sentence. Never translate the word inside braces.
3. Keep names of people and places as they are (Riverstone, Tallgrass Valley, Bayview, Riverlands, Amara, Mina L. ...). Real places use their usual Malay spelling (Mesir, Eropah, Jepun, Kemboja; China, Iraq, Syria, Brazil as in English; Sungai Nil, Sungai Tigris, Sungai Euphrates, Sungai Congo). "Thinkerwell" never changes.
4. Keep numbers as digits where the English has digits. Malay writes thousands with a comma like English (1,000) and decimals with a point.
5. Short labels (buttons, menu items, tabs, chips, picture labels) stay short: about as long as the English.
6. Use the key terms below exactly, everywhere, so the whole site uses the same word for the same thing.
7. Thinkerwell is called a "platform", never a "projek" (it is not a project), and never a charity or nonprofit beyond what the English says.
8. Where you are unsure (a word with no good Malay equivalent, a meaning that may shift, a term to check with a native speaker), still give your best translation and add a short flag note in English saying what to check.
