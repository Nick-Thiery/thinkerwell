# Partner kit

Branch `partner-kit`. Built on 30 September 2026.

What a new partner organisation needs to start a pilot, in English and Bahasa Indonesia, all through the usual messages (`src/i18n/messages/`), with notes and back-translations for the reviewers (`docs/translation/id/notes/ui.json`). The consent form's study parts and the information sheet were added in October 2026 (branch `consent-study-parts`).

## What was built

- **For organisations** at `/organisations` (`src/pages/OrganisationsPage.tsx`). What Thinkerwell is, what partners get, what we ask (a contact person, some devices, a few sessions a week for 4 to 6 weeks, honest feedback), privacy (no names, code cards, parental consent and the learner's own yes), the tools to get ready (the information sheet, the consent form, code cards and the setup checklist), and how to contact us, once there is an address (`CONTACT_EMAIL` in `src/app/contact.ts`; until then the contact card isn't shown, and neither is a placeholder). There is no form, so the page collects nothing. It says plainly that Thinkerwell is a student-led project, not a registered charity.
- **Consent form** at `/educators/consent-form` (`src/pages/educators/ConsentFormPage.tsx`). The one consent form for a pilot (the draft "Pilot Pack" written for HELP had a fuller one; its parts are here now, October 2026), in the language on screen, on two pages in black and white, A4 or Letter. Each page is headed with the form's title, "Page 1 of 2" or "Page 2 of 2", who it is for, and "Pilot code (the organisation fills this in): ______", so either page stands alone.
  - **Page 1, for the parent or guardian:** what the pilot is; what the pilot study involves; what is kept and what isn't; that it is their choice; "Questions? Ask [organisation]" (with the email once there is one) and that Thinkerwell is a student-led project, not a registered charity. Then **two separate questions, each with a Yes and a No box**: "My child can use Thinkerwell in class at [organisation]." and "My child can take part in the pilot study. Their answers are used with a code, never their name." A family can say yes to one and no to the other. Then the child's name, the parent's or guardian's name, the date and a box for a **signature or thumbprint**, and "[Organisation] keeps this form somewhere locked. Thinkerwell never sees it."
  - **Page 2, for staff:** the child's name; **the learner's own answer**, a short script staff read aloud in the learner's language ("We're trying a new learning website called Thinkerwell. … Do you want your answers to be part of the study?"), a Yes and a No box, staff initials and the date, and "If the parent says yes and the learner says no, the learner's no wins."; then the **witness line** for a parent or guardian who can't read or write: "I read the information sheet and this form aloud in ______ (language). The parent or guardian understood and gave the answers on page 1.", with the staff member's name, signature and date.

  Staff can type the organisation's name (up to 60 characters: it is printed nine times, and a longer one would push the form onto a third page), which goes into every place the form names it. Left empty, the form prints a line to write it on. The name is kept in the page only (never saved or put in the address) and marked `translate="no"`. A staff note on screen (not printed) says it is a template to check against their own rules and data protection law, and not legal advice; to give each family the information sheet with it and read both aloud where needed, signing as a witness; that learners aged 18 or older sign for themselves; and that the form doesn't cover online speech-to-text, so it stays off in Settings.
- **Information sheet** at `/educators/information-sheet` (`InformationSheetPage.tsx`), next to the consent form wherever it is linked (For organisations' "Get ready" and the Educators page's "Starting a pilot"). For each family to keep, in the language on screen, on one page in black and white, A4 or Letter: what Thinkerwell is, what will happen ("From [start date] to [end date], your child's class at [organisation] will use Thinkerwell on its tablets and laptops, about [number] times. A teacher from [organisation] leads every session."), the pilot study, what is kept and what isn't, saying yes or no, and whom to ask ("Ask [contact person] at [organisation]", with "or write to Thinkerwell at …" only once `CONTACT_EMAIL` is set), with "Thinkerwell is run by Justin Park and Nick Thiery. It is a student-led project, not a registered charity." Staff can type the organisation's name, the start and end dates, the number of sessions and a contact person, kept in the page only like the form's name; each left empty prints a line to write on. Dates and the number are printed as typed (they are the organisation's words), so nothing is formatted by hand.
- **The promises its consent form makes** (and the information sheet repeats, from the same messages, `pages.pilotStudy` in `src/pages/educators/pilotStudy.tsx`, so the two can't drift apart). These are what the measurement build must keep to, or the form must change first:
  - **What the study involves**, only if the family says yes to the study: in the first and last sessions, 16 short questions (about 15 minutes each time), a few sentences about a made-up town, on paper (about 8 minutes), and choosing a face for each of 5 sentences (about 1 minute); in every session, how long the child uses Thinkerwell, which lessons they open and finish, and their quiz scores. None of it is a test, and nothing depends on the answers.
  - Answers are linked to a code, like HLP-01, never to the child's name.
  - The organisation keeps the list of names and codes on paper. Thinkerwell never sees it.
  - Thinkerwell never asks about family, journey, home country, religion or ethnicity, never asks for UNHCR numbers or any documents, and never collects photos of the child or where they are.
  - What the child writes and records in the lessons stays on the device they use.
  - Only the Thinkerwell team sees the answers; the organisation gets results for the whole group, never for one child.
  - Everything the study keeps is deleted within 6 months after the pilot ends.
  - When a YouTube video plays, YouTube (owned by Google) gets some information, such as the device's internet (IP) address; every video has a written version, and choosing "Read instead" sends nothing to YouTube.
  - Saying no to either question changes nothing else the child does at the organisation; with no to the study the child still uses Thinkerwell in class, and their answers just aren't used. The family can change their mind at any time, even after the pilot, by telling the organisation, and the child's answers are deleted. The child can say no too, and their no wins over a parent's yes.
- **Code cards** at `/educators/code-cards` (`CodeCardsPage.tsx`, logic in `codeCards.ts`). A prefix (2 to 5 letters or digits, capitalised as typed) and a number of learners (1 to 60) make cards HLP-01, HLP-02 …, ten to an A4 page with dashed lines to cut along, then a list of the codes with an empty space for each name, for the organisation to keep. The prefix and number are in the address, so a reload makes the same cards. Nobody types a name here.
- **The Educators page** has a new part, "Starting a pilot", before the other tools. It links For organisations, the information sheet (since October 2026), the consent form, the code cards and the setup checklist. The other tools (the class view and certificates) are now under "Follow your group". The "Class codes instead of names" card links to the code cards too.
- **A footer on every page** (`src/app/SiteFooter.tsx`), with links to For organisations and Credits (`/credits`, added later; the line saying Thinkerwell is a student-led project, not a registered charity, was taken off the footer then). It is hidden when printing. There was no footer before.
- **Set up this device** was already fully translated and printed on one A4 page in both languages. Step 4 now says that learners with code cards type their code as their name.

## Decisions

1. **The consent form describes the pilot's measurement, which isn't built yet.** Today learners' work and progress are not sent to Thinkerwell; the only outside services a learner can reach are YouTube (a video, after a tap) and online speech-to-text (off unless staff turn it on). The For organisations page says so ("We will tell you before anything else changes"), and says consent comes before any learning data is shared. The form's promises follow `docs/research/MEASUREMENT_PLAN.md` (sections 1 to 3 and "Privacy and consent"): data linked to a code, only the team sees it, the organisation gets results for the whole group, and it is deleted within 6 months of the pilot ending. The measurement build must keep to these promises, or the form must change first. Two things to watch there: the plan's in-app events (section 4) also log word counts, Simpler and Listen use, video choices and each quick-check answer, which the form's "how long your child uses it, which lessons they open and finish, and their quiz scores" doesn't plainly cover; and the writing task is marked from paper, so whoever marks it must be someone the form allows to see the answers (today: only the Thinkerwell team).
2. **Two questions, not one** (October 2026). Using Thinkerwell in class and taking part in the study are separate yes/no answers, so a family can say yes to the class and no to the study. The learner's own yes is on the form too (page 2), and their no wins. Learners aged 18 or older sign for themselves (the staff note says so; the form itself doesn't).
3. **"Anonymous" is gone from the form.** It said "only anonymous data … linked to a code". Data linked to a code that the organisation can match to a name isn't anonymous in the strict sense (data protection laws call it pseudonymous), so the form now says what is true: a code, never the name, and Thinkerwell never sees the list. The promise itself (no names reach Thinkerwell) is unchanged. For organisations says "data linked to a code, never a name" too.
4. **Two pages.** The study parts didn't fit on one page at the site's smallest text size (14px, 10.5pt), so the form is two pages, each standing alone. On paper it uses 12mm margins (a named page, `@page tw-consent`, as the certificates do), and the parent's four fields share one row; page 1 still fits Letter in Indonesian with a 60-character name. `e2e/partner-kit.spec.ts` prints both printouts to A4 and Letter in both languages, with the name empty, short and as long as the box allows, and checks the page counts, that no colour is left and that nothing printed is under 14px.
5. **One set of words for both printouts.** The study and the list of what is kept are `pages.pilotStudy` messages, rendered by the same components on the form and the sheet (`pilotStudy.tsx`); a unit test checks they come out the same. "Read instead" in the YouTube promise is the Watch step's own button label, in the language on screen.
6. **Codes as names.** A learner types their code in the name field ("I'm new here"). The new-learner form's optional class code field stays as it is. The card doesn't mention it, to keep the card to one instruction.
7. **Indonesian register.** Every page for adults speaks to them as "Anda": the consent form, For organisations, the Educators pages, teacher guides, setup, code cards and Settings (changed from "kamu" in September 2026). What a learner reads, such as the line on each code card, keeps "kamu". This is flagged for the native reviewers to confirm.
8. **Printing.** Every printout uses `src/pages/print/print.css`: black on white with no colour, no toolbar and no header or footer. `e2e/partner-kit.spec.ts` prints each one in both languages. It checks the page count and that no colour is left.
9. **Indonesian.** Every new or changed string was drafted by AI, back-translated into English by a second AI that saw only the Indonesian, compared with the English and fixed where they differed (two strings), and flagged for the native reviewers in `docs/translation/id/notes/ui.json` (with the back-translation, the English it was made from and what to check). The learner's script uses "kamu"; everything else on the printouts uses "Anda". New key terms (the pilot study, consent form, information sheet, pilot code, thumbprint, witness) are in `docs/translation/id/KEY_TERMS.md`.
