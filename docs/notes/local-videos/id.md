# Indonesian Watch videos: candidates for review

Generated 2026-10-14 for Justin Park and HELP for Refugees. The data is in `id.json` beside this file, in the same shape as a lesson's `watch` fields (with a few review fields added).

**Nobody has watched any of these videos.** Every candidate is a real YouTube video that the oEmbed check accepted (so it exists, is public and can be embedded), and its title, channel and length were read from YouTube's own listing. What each video says is not known beyond its title and the first lines of its description. Every entry therefore says `needsWatching: true`, and the written summaries are worded as "according to the title and description". Please have someone who speaks Indonesian watch each one, with the sound on, before it reaches a learner. Nothing here is wired into the app.

## At a glance

- All 24 lessons have a primary and a backup that passed the oEmbed check (48 videos, none used twice).
- Fit of the primaries, 1 (poor) to 5 (exact): 15 of them 4, 8 of them 3, 1 of them 2. Nothing scored 5, because nothing could be watched. A 4 means "on topic by its title and description".
- Lengths run from 1:01 to 10:04; the number over 10 minutes is 1 (L03).
- Channels: Kok Bisa? supplies 15 of the 48. It is well made and well known, but its language is informal (everyday slang) and several of its videos are 6 to 9 years old. Others come from BRIN, UNICEF Indonesia, BMKG, BNPB, OJK, TVRI, Perpustakaan Nasional, Museum Nasional, Kompas, Mafindo and Nat Geo Indonesia.
- Some candidates come from the Indonesian editions of international brands: BBC News Indonesia (Lesson 18 backup) and Nat Geo Indonesia (Lessons 7 and 10). They are in Indonesian, but the BBC one is not an Indonesian creator, and Nat Geo Indonesia is published by an Indonesian media group. They are marked in the notes.

## How this was done, and its limits

- **Finding videos.** The YouTube search results page was searched in Indonesian for each lesson's topic. From each results page the ID, title, channel, length, age and the first line of the description were read. IDs were only ever taken from those pages, never written from memory.
- **Checking them.** Every ID in `id.json` was sent to YouTube's oEmbed endpoint (`https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<ID>&format=json`) and returned a valid answer. oEmbed returns an error for a video that is private, removed or not embeddable. One ID that failed it (a 404) was dropped. This does not check that the video plays with captions or sound on the pilot laptops, or that the YouTube player's "Error 153" will not appear (the embed code already handles the referrer).
- **Lengths.** `durationSeconds` comes from the listing page, and was compared to a second reading where there was one. Lesson 1's primary (6:40) was read from its watch page.
- **Summaries.** There was no way to read a transcript or the full description (YouTube's watch pages blocked automated reading part-way through), so almost every summary rests on the title and the first lines of the description (`summaryBasis: title-and-description`; one is `description`). The "key points" repeat only what those lines say. They must be replaced after someone watches the video.
- **Questions and "why" lines** (`why`, `beforeQuestion`, `afterQuestion`) are drafts in plain Bahasa Indonesia, written to the style of the existing Indonesian lessons (informal "kamu"). They were not reviewed by a native speaker. Under Thinkerwell's own rule, they must be reviewed before learners see them.
- **Country.** `channelCountry` is `ID` for every entry, because every channel publishes in Indonesia. BBC News Indonesia and Nat Geo Indonesia are noted in `fitNotes`.
- **Not checked at all:** whether the videos have captions in Indonesian, whether they carry ads that YouTube inserts (the embed is `youtube-nocookie.com`, but ads can still play), whether the content stays online, and whether the facts are right. Several videos are 6 to 9 years old.
- **Register.** Kok Bisa? and Zenius use everyday slang. It is popular with Indonesian teenagers, but teachers should decide if that is right for the learners' Indonesian.
